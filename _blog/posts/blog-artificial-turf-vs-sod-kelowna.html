When artificial turf makes sense

  • You water and the lawn still dies. Okanagan sun plus watering restrictions make some lawns a fight you cannot win.
  • You have shade or slope where grass never takes. Turf does not care about shade.
  • You are done spending your weekends mowing.
  • You want a putting green in your backyard.

Done right, you get no watering, no mowing and no brown patches in August.

When real sod makes sense

  • You want real grass under your feet and you do not mind looking after it.
  • Your yard gets the sun grass needs.
  • You are fine watering it through an Okanagan summer.

A real lawn needs your soil prepped first. Grass laid on poor soil with no prep comes up patchy every year, and no amount of water fixes it. What is under the topsoil decides what grows in it.

Base first, for turf and for sod

Turf is a hardscape job wearing a lawn costume. Cheap installs lay it straight on dirt and skip the base and the drainage. It wrinkles, sinks and holds water. Proper turf goes down on a compacted, draining base with clean edges. Sod is the same story with soil: grading and soil first, because planting into ground nobody prepped is money you spend twice.

The honest trade-offs

Turf is low work, not no work. It is a surface you sweep now and then. Real sod is a lawn you look after every week, and watering restrictions can make that hard in a dry summer.
