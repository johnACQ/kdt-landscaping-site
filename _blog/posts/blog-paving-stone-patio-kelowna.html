Your patio sank. Odds are the stones are not to blame.

Sunken corners. Stones sitting higher than the ones beside them. Weeds pushing up through the joints. If that is your patio or driveway, it is almost always a base problem, not a paving stone problem.

Pull the weeds and reset the low stones, and the same spots sink again, because the trouble is under them. Your stones only stay flat if the ground under them does. If you want it done right this time, you want what Heather Rowe described in her review of KDT: "Everything was done with care and finished to a very high standard."

Base first, drainage first

The paving stones are the last layer. What goes under them decides whether your patio is still flat in year ten:

  • Soft soil dug out, not covered over.
  • A base packed down in layers, so nothing settles later.
  • A slope built into the surface, so rain and snow melt run away from your house, not at it.
  • Solid edges, so your stones cannot creep apart.

Hardscaping is drainage first, looks second. Get those right and picking your stone is the easy part.

Driveways take more of a beating

A paving stone driveway works the same way, with more riding on it. It carries the weight of your vehicles every day, so a weak base shows up faster. Same rule, more care. You can see driveways, walls and full builds on the Kelowna landscaping page.
